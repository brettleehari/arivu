Arivu Privacy Policy

App: Arivu (Android) · Developer: [DEVELOPER_NAME] · Effective: [EFFECTIVE_DATE] · Applies to version 0.1.0 and later until this page changes

The short version

  • Arivu has no permission to use the internet. Android does not let it send anything anywhere.
  • What you type and what Arivu writes is stored only on your phone, in the app's private storage. It is not included in phone backups or phone-to-phone transfers.
  • We do not collect, receive, sell or share your messages. There is no account, no advertising, no analytics and no crash-reporting software in the app.
  • If you choose to report a reply, your own email app sends it. The draft contains that reply and your note, and you see it before you send. We receive only what you send.
  • Google Play, which you use to install Arivu, has its own privacy policy.

1. What Arivu is

Arivu is an offline writing and comprehension assistant. A small language model (Qwen3 0.6B) is installed together with the app and runs entirely on your phone. It works on text you give it: rewriting, shortening, explaining, summarising and drafting.

2. Arivu cannot connect to the internet

Android apps must ask for the INTERNET permission before they can use any network. Arivu does not ask for it, and it does not ask for any permission to see your network state. Our release check (tools/check_manifest.sh in the source code) fails if any part of the app adds a network permission.

Arivu does ask Android for these permissions. None of them show a pop-up, and none of them give access to the internet or to your personal data:

PermissionWhy
FOREGROUND_SERVICESo a reply can keep being written for a short time if you switch to another app.
REQUEST_DELETE_PACKAGESUsed only on a phone that cannot run Arivu well, to open Android's screen for uninstalling Arivu itself. Arivu uses it for no other app, and Android always asks you to confirm before anything is removed.
…DYNAMIC_RECEIVER_NOT_EXPORTED_PERMISSIONAdded automatically by Google's AndroidX library. Only Arivu itself holds it; it stops other apps from sending Arivu internal messages.

You can check this yourself on the Google Play listing ("App permissions"), in your phone's app settings, or in the public source code.

3. What is stored on your phone

WhatWhereHow long
Your conversation: the messages you send, the replies, the time each was created, why a reply stopped, and whether you reported a reply One file, conversation.json, in Arivu's private app storage. Other apps cannot read it on a normal Android phone. Until you uninstall Arivu or clear its storage in Android settings. Version 0.1.0 has no in-app button to clear the conversation.
Temporary and damaged copies of that file Same private folder. A temporary copy is used while saving. If the file is ever damaged, Arivu keeps only the newest damaged copy there instead of overwriting it, and starts a new conversation. Same as above.
A note that your phone passed Arivu's compatibility check (app version and total memory size) Arivu's private app settings Same as above. Used so the check does not run on every launch.

Arivu turns off Android backup for the whole app (allowBackup="false") and excludes all its storage from both cloud backup and phone-to-phone transfer. Your conversation will not appear in your Google account backup and will not move to a new phone.

Arivu does not encrypt the file itself. It is protected by Android's app sandbox and by your phone's own storage encryption and screen lock. Anyone who can unlock your phone and open Arivu can read the conversation.

Like most Android apps, Arivu writes short technical messages to the phone's system log (for example, when the model is loaded or memory is freed). The system log stays on your phone, and Android does not let ordinary apps read other apps' log messages.

4. What we collect

Nothing, through the app. Arivu contains no analytics, advertising, tracking, crash-reporting or payment software, and it cannot send data because it cannot use the internet. We never see your messages, and they are not used to train any AI model.

5. Reporting a harmful reply

There are two ways to report. Both use your own email app (or, if you have no email app, Android's share menu, where you choose the app). Arivu cannot send anything itself.

Report under a reply. Every reply Arivu writes has a Report button. It opens a small screen inside Arivu where you choose what is wrong (Offensive, Harmful, Wrong in a dangerous way, or Something else) and can add a note. When you tap "Open email", Arivu:

  • marks that reply as "Reported" in the conversation stored on your phone, and
  • opens a draft email to [CONTACT_EMAIL] with the subject "Arivu: report of harmful output", containing the reason you chose, your note (if any), the full text of that reply, and the Arivu version number.

Only the reply you chose is put in the draft. Your own messages and the rest of the conversation are not added. You can read and change the draft, and nothing is sent until you press send in your email or messaging app. That app, not Arivu, sends it. If you do not send it, the reply still shows as "Reported" on your phone only.

General report. The About screen has a "Report a reply" button. It opens a draft email to the same address with the same subject, a short blank form ("Paste the reply here", "What was wrong with it") and the Arivu version number. You choose what to paste in.

If you send a report, we receive what is in it (the reply text, your reason and note, anything you added) and whatever your email or messaging service includes, such as your email address or phone number. We use reports only to understand harmful replies, to make Arivu safer, and to reply to you if needed. We do not sell or share reports. We keep them for [REPORT_RETENTION] and then delete them. You can ask us to see or delete your report sooner by writing to [CONTACT_EMAIL]. Your email or messaging provider handles the message under its own privacy policy.

6. Copying text and your keyboard

When you tap Copy, the text goes to your phone's clipboard, like any copied text. Arivu asks your keyboard app not to learn from what you type in Arivu, but Arivu cannot see or control what your keyboard app does. Other apps you paste into, and your keyboard app, work under their own privacy policies.

7. Google Play and Android

You install and update Arivu through Google Play. Google may collect information about your device and your download under Google's Privacy Policy. Google gives developers summary statistics, such as the number of installs by country and device model, and, from people who chose to share usage and diagnostics with Google, crash and performance statistics. We use these only to find phones and problems where Arivu does not work well. We do not receive your messages from Google, because Arivu never sends them anywhere.

Installing and updating from Google Play uses data. Once installed, Arivu itself uses none.

8. Children

Arivu is not designed for children and we do not knowingly collect information from anyone, including children. The app itself collects nothing. If a child has sent us a report email, write to [CONTACT_EMAIL] and we will delete it.

9. Your choices and rights

  • Delete your conversation: uninstall Arivu, or open Android Settings → Apps → Arivu → Storage → Clear storage.
  • Check our claims: the full source code is public at [REPO_URL].
  • Reports you sent us: ask to see or delete them at [CONTACT_EMAIL]. Depending on where you live (for example under Nigeria's NDPA, Kenya's Data Protection Act, Brazil's LGPD, the Philippines' Data Privacy Act or the EU GDPR), you may have further rights over that information, and you can complain to your data protection authority.

10. Changes to this policy

If a future version of Arivu changes what it stores or sends, we will update this page before that version is released and change the effective date at the top. A version that needed the internet would be a different app from the one described here, and we would say so plainly in the Google Play listing.
